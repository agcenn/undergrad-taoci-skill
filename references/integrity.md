# Integrity and Evidence Rules

## Student facts

Treat user-provided materials as the source of truth for personal facts. Do not infer missing GPA, rank, publications, awards, internships, project metrics, or time commitment.

When useful information is missing:
- omit it, or
- use an explicit placeholder if the user asked for a template.

## Mentor facts

For current research, recruiting, lab membership, and undergraduate participation, prefer public primary sources.

Internally label claims as:
- VERIFIED — directly supported by a reliable source
- INFERRED — reasonable synthesis from verified facts
- UNKNOWN — no adequate public evidence

Do not convert INFERRED or UNKNOWN into confident facts in the email.

## Paper-reading honesty

The assistant reading a paper is not the same as the student having read it.

Unless the user confirms personal reading/understanding, avoid first-person claims such as:
- “I carefully read...”
- “After studying your paper...”
- “I was deeply inspired by the experiments...”

Instead describe the visible research connection:
> I noticed your group has recently worked on ...

## Undergraduate participation

Evidence of undergraduates in a lab does not prove the professor is currently recruiting. Phrase accordingly.

## Sources in outputs

When performing mentor analysis, give source links/citations when the host supports them. Do not put citation clutter into the final email unless the user explicitly wants it.
