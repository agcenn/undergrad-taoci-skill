# Outreach Strategy

Before drafting, choose one main message. A good undergraduate outreach email is not a compressed CV.

## The one-sentence strategy

Complete this internally:

> I am contacting this mentor because **[specific current research anchor]** connects to my **[real experience]**, and I am asking for **[specific undergraduate research opportunity]**.

If this sentence cannot be written honestly, the match is not ready.

## Research anchors

Good anchors:
- a current research problem
- a recent project/system
- a representative paper the student actually inspected
- a lab theme that is demonstrably active

Weak anchors:
- “your outstanding research”
- “your lab is world-class”
- generic AI / CS enthusiasm
- name-dropping papers without understanding

## Student evidence hierarchy

Use the strongest relevant evidence, not the most prestigious-looking item:

1. directly relevant project/research
2. relevant technical project
3. relevant course + implementation
4. adjacent engineering/research experience
5. general achievement only if it supports the narrative

## Ask

Prefer a concrete, low-friction ask:
- possibility of participating in undergraduate research
- opportunity to discuss a possible entry task
- whether the professor is open to an undergraduate joining/learning with the group

Avoid asking for vague “guidance” with no research context.
