# Email Review Rubric

Review the email before presenting the final version.

## Hard fails — must revise

- fabricated student fact
- unsupported claim that the professor is recruiting
- unsupported “I read/studied your paper” statement
- no mentor-specific research anchor
- obvious name-swap mass-mail template
- excessive flattery replacing substantive fit
- request is unclear

## Quality dimensions

Score internally 0–2 each:

1. Truthfulness
2. Mentor specificity
3. Student-to-research connection
4. Undergraduate-appropriate positioning
5. Clear request
6. Brevity
7. Natural tone
8. Low mass-mail smell

Interpretation:
- 14–16: ready
- 11–13: revise once
- 8–10: substantial rewrite
- below 8: rebuild strategy

Do not expose a fake precision score unless the user asks for it. Use the rubric to improve the draft.

## Mass-mail smell test

Temporarily remove the professor's name and paper/lab name.

Ask:
> Could this exact email be sent unchanged to ten unrelated professors?

If yes, it is not ready.

## AI-style smell

Remove:
- stacked adjectives
- repetitive “非常/十分/深感”
- abstract claims with no evidence
- oversized transition phrases
- unnatural certainty about research the student just discovered

## Review output

If the user asks to review an existing email:
1. list only the most important 3–5 issues
2. provide a complete revised email
